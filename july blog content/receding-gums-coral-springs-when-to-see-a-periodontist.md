## **What Causes Receding Gums? Coral Springs Patients Ask This a Lot**

Gum recession happens when the gum tissue surrounding your teeth pulls back, exposing more of the tooth or its root. At Coral Springs Smiles, the most common causes we see include:

* Untreated gum disease (gingivitis or periodontitis)  
* Aggressive brushing or a hard-bristled toothbrush  
* Genetics — some patients are simply more prone to thin or fragile gum tissue  
* Teeth grinding or clenching  
* Tobacco use  
* Misaligned teeth putting uneven pressure on the gums

Because Florida's humid climate and busy lifestyles can make it easy to skip regular dental visits, many Coral Springs patients don't notice recession until it's already moderate. That's exactly why routine checkups with a gum specialist near Coral Springs matter — small changes are much easier to treat than advanced ones.

## **Warning Signs You Shouldn't Ignore**

Not every case of gum recession comes with obvious pain, which is part of what makes it tricky. Watch for:

* Teeth that appear longer than before  
* Sensitivity to hot, cold, or sweet foods  
* A visible notch or line where the gum meets the tooth  
* Gums that bleed easily when brushing or flossing  
* Loose-feeling teeth

If any of these sound familiar, it's worth scheduling an exam with Dr. Anand rather than waiting for symptoms to worsen. Gum tissue doesn't grow back on its own, and early intervention gives you far more treatment options.

## **When Should You Actually See a Periodontist?**

Not every patient with slightly receded gums needs specialized periodontal treatment — but you should schedule a visit with our Coral Springs gum specialist if:

* Recession has progressed noticeably over the past year  
* You have persistent bad breath or a bad taste that won't go away  
* Your teeth feel loose or your bite has changed  
* You've been told you have moderate-to-severe periodontitis  
* You have gum recession around an existing dental implant

Dr. Anand's periodontics fellowship training means she can identify whether your case needs a conservative approach or more advanced intervention — without unnecessary treatment or unnecessary worry.
